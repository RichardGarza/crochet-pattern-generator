Path B steps 1–3 and the seam of step 5 (§2.10.7), pure and synchronous, in `src/core/meshtools`:

Pipeline (`geodesicRows`): `remeshForPathB` (voxelize at a voxel = target edge / 0.92, MC, 10 Taubin pairs, keep
the largest component) → `surfaceMesh` → `chooseSeed` → `HeatSolver.geodesic` (t = (mean edge)², doubled ≤ 6 times
while two critical points are ≤ 2 edges apart) → `rowLevels` (N = max(2, roundHalfUp(max φ / hS)), levels k·hEff,
k = 1 … N − 1) → `isolineLoops` per level → first level with > 1 loop ⇒ `needsSplit`; else the seam
(`seamPath`, Dijkstra seed → argmax φ) and each row's loop rotated to start on the edge where the seam crosses it.

Why a direct solver for the heat step (`sparse.ts`): the heat kernel decays like e^(−d/√t), i.e. e^(−1) per edge
at t = (mean edge)², so on the r = 2 in sphere u falls to ~1e-40 at the antipode. Jacobi-PCG at 1e-8 relative
residual returns noise there: the same sphere with a PCG heat step gave a **44%** mean error (and exact zeros in u)
against 0.94% with the factorization. The Cholesky (geometric nested dissection, up-looking CSparse algorithm,
symbolic analysis once per mesh, numeric refactor per t) keeps the far field; 638 k nonzeros for 14 k vertices.
Even a factorization underflows past ≈ 745 edges (e^(−745) is below the double range), so t starts at
max((mean edge)², (D/600)²), D = the farthest edge-path distance from the seed (review finding 1).

The Poisson solve defaults to the same Cholesky machinery on L pinned at the seed (L + L_ss·e_s·e_sᵀ; with a
right-hand side summing to 0 its solution is exactly Lφ = b with φ_s = 0), factored once per call, so each t doubling
costs one heat factorization instead of a full PCG run (the 6-doubling path on 30 k vertices went from ≈ 2.4 s to
the figure below). `poisson: 'pcg'` keeps the §2.10.7 wording (Jacobi-PCG, rel. tol 1e-8, ≤ 2000 iterations,
ε·M with ε·M_ii ≈ 1e-10·L_ii, warm start across doublings, direct fallback if it does not converge); the two agree
to < 1e-5 in (tested).

## Public API (T5.1)

All lengths inches; volumes are positive inside, `field[x + nx·(y + ny·z)]`, sample (x, y, z) at the POINT
`origin + voxel·(x, y, z)` (the Step 0 convention). Nothing uses `Math.random` or `Date`; every function is
deterministic (byte-identical outputs on repeated runs; tested for voxelize and merge).

```ts
const g = geodesicRows(mesh, { hS, targetEdge: pathBTargetEdge(w, h), seed: part.crochet?.seed, attach, up });

// step 6: DTW couple pts[k−1] → pts[k], transducer (T5.3)
```

## Deviations from the spec, with reasons

1. **Flatten plane through the mean surface point under the brush**, not literally "through the brush center".
   The editor puts the brush center on the surface; on a convex patch the tangent plane there lies entirely above
   the surface, so `min(f, −dist_P)` would remove nothing and Flatten would never flatten a curved part (the
   acceptance "flatten lowers the height above the plane" could not hold). `brushPlane` takes the φ-weighted mean of
   the near-surface samples moved onto the surface along their normals (the usual sculpting definition); on a flat
   surface it coincides with the brush center (tested: < 0.05 voxel change on a slab). Normal = mean surface normal
   under the brush, as specified.
2. **Nearest vertex by a k-d tree, not a BVH.** "Nearest vertex" is a point query; three-mesh-bvh answers nearest
   triangle. The k-d tree is exact, ties → lowest index, no three.js in the worker path. (A first uniform-grid
   version took 20 s on a cut, whose cap vertices are far from every source vertex; the tree takes milliseconds.)
3. **Merging parts that touch without overlapping (0 < gap ≤ 0.1 in).** §2.9.8 accepts them but `max(f_a, f_b)` of
   two separate solids is two pieces. When the union meshes to more than one piece, every linked pair that shares no
   lattice sample gets a bridge: a capsule between the two nearest surface points (from the gap search, projected
   onto each surface), each end one voxel inside its part, radius max(1.5 voxels, gap). Only that neighborhood
   changes. (The first version used a morphological closing of the whole union; the review showed it fails on sharp,
   small contacts — a cone tip 0.05–0.099 in above a 0.3 in sphere — and fills creases everywhere, +1% on the
   teddy.) `bridgeIn` reports the radius (0 when not needed: the plain teddy is unchanged); a pair that still does
   not join is `bridge-failed`, not "do not touch".
4. **Merge geometry is in model space.** The frozen result has no position, and the merged part is unrotated; T6
   calls `recenterMesh` for the bbox-centered form (§3.5.1) and the position (request 2).
5. **Labels need the palette.** Labels index the model palette, but `MeshApi.merge` / `fromPart` receive no palette;
   the kernels take `paletteIds` (without it primitives contribute 255 = unknown; `partId` is always set). See
   request 1.
6. **Convert falls back to the analytic SDF for open builder solids** (open cylinder, torus arc, lathe not closed on
   the axis): their tessellation is not closed, so the parity scan has no inside. Closed ones are voxelized from the
   builder geometry as specified (welded by position to test closedness: three.js duplicates seams and poles).
7. **Convert keeps the part's local frame** (so the converted part keeps position, rotation and attach). For a lathe
   the origin stays at its base, not the mesh's bbox center; `recenterMesh` gives the centered form if T6 wants it.
8. **Gap measure.** `pairGap` minimizes `max(−f_a, 0) + max(−f_b, 0)` (= the gap along the segment between the
   closest points) from the best lattice sample with a pattern search on the continuous SDFs, so all part kinds
   (analytic, stored volume, voxelized buffer) are measured alike. The ellipsoid SDF is a lower bound outside, so a
   gap can only come out smaller (more lenient); teddy at +0.2 / +0.3 in: 0.0514 / 0.1513 here vs `surfaceGap`
   0.0535 / 0.1532.

9. **Flat parts: converted mesh ≠ analytic prism.** The builder bevels flat shapes (§3.4.1) while the analytic SDF and
   `partVolume` ignore the bevel (Step 0, s0b-model); a converted flat part follows the builder geometry (within 0.1%
   of its volume, 13–31% more than `partVolume`), so "convert, then merge" and "merge the primitive" differ for flat
   parts. A Step 0 inconsistency, noted for integration.
10. **Merging a mesh part refuses an open or too-thin buffer** (`bad-mesh`): the parity sign of an open mesh is
    meaningless, and a broken solid must not be merged silently. The voxelizer itself only reports `oddColumns`.

11. **(T5.2) Heat step by sparse Cholesky, not an iterative solver; t floored at (D/600)² on very long parts.** The spec names Jacobi-PCG only for the Poisson
    step; the heat step needs the far field of u that a residual-tolerance solver cannot deliver (44% vs 0.94% mean
    error on the r = 2 in sphere, see the sprint log). Nested-dissection Cholesky, deterministic, no dependency.
12. **(T5.2) Poisson by a pinned direct solve by default**, not Jacobi-PCG with ε·M: same solution (tested to
    < 1e-5 in), but factored once per call, which keeps the t-doubling path inside the §5.8 budget. The specified
    PCG stays available (`poisson: 'pcg'`, with a direct fallback when it does not converge in 2000 iterations).
13. **(T5.2) Re-mesh refuses a part with < 8 voxels³ inside** (`bad-mesh`): the voxelizer leaves slivers around
    lattice samples lying exactly on a flat sheet, which would otherwise come out as a "part".
14. **(T5.2) Re-mesh vertex cap (30 k)**: the target edge min(w, h)/3 is coarsened (`coarsened: true`) when the part
    would exceed 30 k vertices (a 6 in ball at fingering gauge would be 90 k vertices and 3 s for this share alone).
    Rows are still ≥ 1.8 edges apart at fingering gauge; isoline lengths stay within 1% on spheres at that edge.
15. **(T5.2) Root seed on a flat bottom:** "lowest vertex" lands on the rim of a flat base after MC + Taubin; the seed
    is the vertex nearest the area-weighted centroid of the connected patch within one mean edge of the lowest height
    (on a round bottom that is the lowest point).
16. **(T5.2) `remesh: false` checks the winding** (`bad-mesh` for inconsistent winding, an inside-out mesh is
    flipped), and faces with area < 1e-10·(longest edge)² are left out of the operators (mass only).

| Where | Reading |
|---|---|
| §2.9.8 "N = 96" for the voxelizer | N samples along the longest side of the mesh's box, 2 extra samples beyond it per side (the band fits), equal spacing on every axis |

| A column with an odd number of crossings (open mesh) | Its last crossing is dropped; `stats.oddColumns` counts them; never throws |
| A sample within 0.01 voxel of the surface | Either side (both MC and the tests accept it) |

## Requests for integration

1. **`MeshApi.merge` / `MeshApi.fromPart` (src/types/workers.ts, additive):** `merge(parts, o?: { N?: number;
   paletteIds?: string[] })` and `fromPart(part, o?: { paletteIds?: string[]; N?: number })`. Labels are palette
   indices (§2.11.1) and neither call receives the palette today, so a primitive's colors cannot be carried (the
   kernels already take `paletteIds`).
2. **`MeshApi.merge` result (additive):** an optional `position?: Vec3` (or a sentence in §2.9.8) saying the mesh and
   `sdf` are in model space for an unrotated part; `recenterMesh` gives the §3.5.1 bbox-centered form.
3. **`MeshApi.redoSculpt?(undoId)` (additive, optional):** the editor has ⇧⌘Z; the kernel's `SculptSession.redo`
   exists, the worker API has no entry for it.
4. **§2.9.8 text:** (a) flatten plane = through the mean surface point under the brush (deviation 1); (b) "BVH" →
   "nearest vertex (k-d tree)"; (c) the bridging closing for touching, non-overlapping parts (deviation 3);
   (d) undo is linear (latest stroke first); (e) convert falls back to the analytic SDF for open builder solids.
5. **Sculpt X symmetry across the model's plane:** `MeshApi.sculpt`'s stroke has only `mirrorX: boolean`, which the
   kernel applies at the part-local x = 0. For an off-center or rotated mesh part (an arm) the model's symmetry plane
   is elsewhere in the part's frame; an optional `mirrorPlane?: { point; normal }` on the stroke (additive) would let
   the editor say so (the kernel already takes it).
6. **Step 0 (s0b-model):** flat parts' analytic SDF / `partVolume` ignore the builder's bevel (deviation 9), so a
   converted flat part is 13–31% larger than the primitive the other kernels see.
7. **(T5.2) `MeshApi.pathB` seed and attachment:** the request carries `partId` and `frame` but not the part's
   `crochet.seed` or the attachment point, which step 1's seed rule needs. Either add `seed?: Vec3` and
   `attach?: Vec3[]` (part-local) to the request, or have `ami.worker` fill `frame` with them; T5.3 can otherwise
   only use the root rule (lowest vertex) or `frame.startPole` along `frame.axis`.
8. **(T5.2) §2.10.7 text:** name the heat-step solver (sparse Cholesky) and the t floor (deviation 11), the Poisson
   solver (deviation 12), the vertex cap (14), the flat-bottom seed (15); define "adjacent critical points" (≤ 2
   edges apart — the review notes "adjacent" could also read 1 hop; 2 catches the spurious max–saddle pairs that sit
   one vertex apart) and `needsSplit.loops` (loop lengths); fix the sign: with L PSD the system is Lφ = −∇·X.
9. **(T5.2) For T5.3:** warn when `remesh.components > 1` (the re-mesh keeps the largest piece) or
   `remesh.oddColumns > 0` (open input); the parity voxelizer turns overlapping unmerged shells into union minus
   lens (inherited from §2.9.8; nonzero winding would fix it).
