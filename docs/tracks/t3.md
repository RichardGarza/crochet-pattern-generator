  when transformers.js is first imported (lazily, with a retry after a failed import).

## Deviations and ambiguities resolved

1. **Background model ("`quantize(…, 3)`").** T1's quantizer is a stub and is built for chart palettes, so masks use
   their own deterministic clustering: start from the mean of the opaque border-band samples, add the farthest sample
   as a new center while it is more than τ/2 from every center (≤ 3), Lloyd after each step. A cluster other than the
   largest is kept only if it holds ≥ 5% of the band **and** appears on ≥ 3 sides of the photo with ≥ 3% of each
   side's band samples. Without the side rule an object cut by an edge of the photo (15% of the band in a test)
   became a background cluster and the flood fill ate the whole object, silently (the mask then did not even touch
   the border, so no guard fired). A wall (top, left, right) or a floor (bottom, left, right) reaches three sides;
   an object cut by one or two edges (a corner; top and bottom) does not. An object cut by three edges can still
   pass the rule; when the mask then comes out empty, the ladder is re-run with the main cluster only, which brings
   the object back with `W_MASK_BORDER`. Cost: a floor seen only in one corner of the photo (two sides) is not
   background; the border guard fires and the brush fixes it.
2. **Band width.** `band` is a fraction of the shorter side: `max(1, round(0.04·min(w, h)))` px (15 px on 512 × 384).
3. **`keepHoles`.** In the §2.9.1 pseudo-code the background is the flood from the border, so `not(isBg)` never has
   holes and `fillHoles` alone could never keep a mug handle open. With `keepHoles` on, every background-like pixel is
   background (connected or enclosed); background-colored detail inside the object then becomes a hole too, which is
   why the default is off (research 04 §12). Off: as specified (connected background only, then fill holes).
4. **Shadow heuristic, made safer.** As specified (bottom 25% of the object's box, darker than the local background
   by more than 0.10 in toe'd OKLab L, chroma within 0.06 of it), plus three guards so a dark or gray toy keeps its
   bottom: the pixel is at most 0.50 darker (a cast shadow keeps some light), it is 4-connected to the background
   through shadow-like pixels, and it is not within ΔEOKr2 0.07 of the object's own colors in the strip (15% of the box
   height) just above the band. Measured: a cast shadow at 40% light is removed (IoU 0.97+ vs the object, 0.93−
   without the heuristic); black, gray and taupe toys on a white floor keep IoU > 0.98. "Local background" = mean of
   the background pixels in the band's rows (else the main cluster). Units: L is toe(L) (tracks CIELAB L*/100), chroma
   is OKLab √(a² + b²).
5. **Order of the steps.** downscale → features → clusters → flood → largest component → shadow → `refineMask`
   (locks → largest component → fill holes → open(1) → close(closeR) → locks). The spec's "re-run the last 3 steps"
   after a brush edit is `refineMask` on the stored `raw` mask; painted pixels always win (applied before and after the
   cleanup), so an erased gap stays open and a painted detached part stays. Changing `keepHoles` re-runs
   `classicalMask` (it changes the flood).
6. **Morphology** uses exact disc structuring elements through `edt2d` (pixel centers within r). The photo frame is not
   background, so a mask touching the frame is not eroded from it. Opening with r = 1 rounds square corners
   (a 20 × 20 square loses its 4 corner pixels).
7. **Transparency.** Pixels with alpha < ½ (after the downscale) are background wherever connected to the border and
   are not sampled for the background model.
8. **Alignment units.** `align.dx`, `align.dy` are world units (object heights) along the view's image right and image
   up; `align.scale` enlarges the view's outline in the world (px/unit = automatic ÷ scale, so 1.1 = 10% larger;
   it does not change `extents`, which come from the automatic scales as in §2.9.2); `rot90` counts quarter turns **clockwise** (a photo app's "rotate right") and `mirror` flips
   left ↔ right after the rotation. These were not pinned down by §2.9.2 or the frozen `PhotoView.align` type.
9. **Several views of one plane.** X (or Z) is the mean of `box.w / box.h` over the front/back (or left/right) views.
   A top/bottom view takes `√(sx·sz)` when X and Z are both known from other views (mismatch warning above 8%),
   otherwise it sets the missing axis from the known one (front + top, or side + top). Two views with the same label
   are both used (united) with `W_VIEW_DUPLICATE`. `E_VIEWS` when no view shows the height or X, Y and Z are not all
   constrained (one photo is the single-image path); views with an empty mask are skipped with `W_VIEW_EMPTY` (a
   warning: the alignment goes on without them). View ids must be unique (`RangeError`).
10. **Consistency IoU** is computed in world space on the N × N plane grid (N = 128, the default build resolution),
    from the signs of the signed-distance tables: per view, its own table > 0 vs the silhouette of the separable hull
    of all views (pairs united). The grid is cubic with 10% padding per side (research 04 §1); T3.2 may refine the
    grid when it builds the volume.
11. **The photo frame is outside.** Each view mask is padded with one background pixel before the signed EDT, so a
    mask cut by the frame (or filling it) has a finite field whose zero level lies at the photo edge; beyond the photo
    the field is clamped to the edge and lowered by the distance to the image. (The Step 0 EDT does not treat the
    frame as a boundary; without the padding a full-frame mask was +∞ everywhere and a cut-off view stayed inside past
    the grid.)
12. **Worker self-tests outside the frozen APIs.** `MlApi` and `GeomApi` have no method that can show "an ORT session
    was created" or "manifold initialised", so each worker exposes one extra method (`ortSelfTest`,
    `manifoldSelfTest`) next to its frozen API; the client (`workers/client.ts`) does not see them. The e2e spec
    spawns the real worker modules with the URL Vite gives them (`/src/workers/x.worker.ts?worker_file&type=module`)
    and wraps them with comlink loaded from `/node_modules/comlink/dist/esm/comlink.mjs`.
13. **The ORT session goes through transformers.js** (`PreTrainedModel.from_pretrained`, model type `custom`), not a
    direct `onnxruntime-web` import: `onnxruntime-web` is only a transitive dependency, and this is the code path
    (fetch → `ensureWasmLoaded` with `useWasmCache` → `InferenceSession.create` → the ORT wasm import) that depth and
    SlimSAM will take, i.e. exactly the path that failed under Vite 8 before the §5.4 fix.
14. **Identity model location.** `src/core/recon/__tests__/fixtures/identity/onnx/model.onnx` (T3-owned, the folder
    layout transformers.js expects for a local model `identity`). The 111 bytes were written by a hand-coded protobuf
    encoder (ModelProto { ir_version 8, producer "crochet-pattern-generator", graph { node Identity x → y; inputs/outputs
    float32 [n] }, opset_import { "", 13 } }); `identityModel.test.ts` checks that structure byte by byte, and the
    model was also run with onnxruntime-node (output = input) before committing.
15. **Every view mask is cleaned in `alignViews`** (§2.9.2 "fill holes + close(2 px) on every mask"), whatever made
    it (classical ladder, brush, SlimSAM later); `keepHoles` skips the fill.

## Review (T3.1)

An independent reviewer checked the sprint against §2.9.1, §2.9.2, §5.4 and §6.3 and probed it with adversarial
inputs (degenerate sizes, bad align parameters, duplicate labels and ids, top-only sets, objects cut by edges,
low contrast). Fixed from its findings: objects cut by two or three edges of the photo were taken for background
(3-side rule + empty-mask fallback, deviation 1, new tests); a full-frame or cut-off view gave an unbounded field
(frame padding, deviation 11); `viewConsistency` threw with no aligned views; duplicate view ids gave wrong IoUs (now
a `RangeError`); the e2e ORT check was circular (it now asserts the requests: `/ort/` pair and the model from the
dev server, nothing to other origins); `align.scale` shrank the outline (now ÷); masks are cleaned in `alignViews`
(deviation 15); `orientMask` validates `rot90`; labels are checked with `Object.hasOwn`; `mirror` must be a
boolean; `E_VIEW_EMPTY` became the warning `W_VIEW_EMPTY`; the test silhouettes are rendered from a §2.9.2 table
written out independently of `align.ts`. Left as is: `ortSelfTest` changes the global transformers.js `env` for its
duration (test-only; documented); the e2e spec loads comlink from `/node_modules/comlink/dist/esm/comlink.mjs`.

## Deviations and ambiguities resolved (T3.2)

1. **Build paths.** Several views that constrain X, Y, Z → hull + front rounding (κ = `MULTI_VIEW_KAPPA` = 1, as
   §2.9.3 says; `settings.kappa` is the single-image slider and is not used here). Views on ONE plane that shows the
   height (front + back, left + right; also several photos of which only one has a mask) → the inflation of the
   united silhouette with `settings.kappa`, built directly in the object frame from the alignment (§2.9.4: "adding a
   back photo turns the project into a two-view hull"; front + back alone do not constrain Z, so alignment's
   `E_VIEWS` is not fatal there). One view → the single-image path in the photo frame, turned by
   `settings.photoView` (not by `PhotoView.label`; F3 stores the same choice in both). Top/bottom only → `E_VIEWS`.
2. **Local thickness is exact.** "Paint ridge discs in increasing radius order" needs a ridge test; the exact one
   (no neighbor disc contains this one) marks almost every pixel of a smooth shape on a pixel grid, and painting
   all those discs took 2.3 s at 512². With a slack (a disc that misses containment by < 0.35 of a step counts as
   contained) only ~100–1000 discs are ridge discs, but rim pixels lost up to 20 px of R_loc (visible notches). The
   fix keeps both: a ridge disc is painted whole, and a contained-with-slack disc paints only the part its larger
   neighbor's disc can miss — the far cap of its outer ring (`|p − q| > r − s·step` and `(p − q)·u < s·step −
   (1 − s)(r − s·step)`, derived in the code). Discs are closed and compared through whole-number squared radii
   (`round(d²)`), so a pixel exactly on a rim is inside (the first version decided those by float32 rounding: up
   to 1.4 px of R on 2–60 rim pixels, found in review). The result equals an integer brute force over every
   pixel's disc on random blobs and on adversarial masks (single pixel, 1–3 px lines, checkerboard, ring,
   frame-cut, full frame), in ≈ 100 ms for a 440 × 320 px ellipse. Painting with `max` replaces the sort.
3. **T and the hull use different conventions on purpose** (integration task 3): hull tables `'boundary'`, T from
   `'samples'`. T is computed on the mask reduced by a whole factor so that a grid voxel spans about 2 mask pixels
   (`T_PIXELS_PER_VOXEL`; a block is inside when ≥ half of it is), then sampled bilinearly onto the plane grid
   (clamped at the photo edge, 0 outside the mask), front ∪ back united by max like the sd tables. At full mask
   resolution a full-frame 512² mask took 0.35 s per view and pushed N = 128 builds to 1.0–1.1 s (review); T is
   sampled onto the grid anyway, so the reduction only moves the half-pixel rim bias of `'samples'` to half a
   reduced pixel (≤ ¼ voxel). The acceptance tests of local thickness run at full resolution.
4. **z_c** is the midpoint of the sub-voxel zero crossings next to the first and last inside samples of each ray
   (not the sample indices), so z_c does not step by whole voxels between neighboring rays.
4a. **Front rounding keeps the depth of deep objects (a deviation from the literal §2.9.3 formula).** T assumes a
   round cross-section (depth = width), so `min(f_hull, κT − |z − z_c|)` cut a fish or a dachshund seen head-on
   (0.4 wide, 2 long) to 0.45 of its height instead of 2.2, with side/top IoU 0.2 and no warning (review). The bound
   is now `κT − |z − z_c|/s` with one stretch s ≥ 1 per build: the median, over the thickest rays (T ≥ 0.9·max T),
   of the hull's half-depth over κT. Cross-sections stay the round ones, stretched along z (exact for an
   ellipsoid: the fish comes out 2.20 / 2.22). Sphere and teddy give s ≈ 1.00–1.01, i.e. the spec formula; their
   results did not change (teddy at N = 128: depth 3.4744 in before and after). `frontRounding(…, { preserveDepth:
   false })` is the literal formula.
5. **`cleanVolume` values.** Dropped components get `−max(|f|, 10⁻³ voxel)`, filled cavities `+max(|f|, 10⁻³
   voxel)` (never an exact 0, which marching cubes reads as inside; ±∞/NaN become ±1 voxel). The closing of
   "Merge touching parts" uses two exact EDTs with radius `MERGE_TOUCHING_FRACTION·(N − 1)` ≥ 1 voxel (2 voxels at
   128, 1 at 64: the same world radius at both resolutions); newly inside samples get a small positive value.
6. **Thin features** ("thinner than 2 voxels"): erosion with the 6-neighbor cross (slabs ≤ 2 samples thick
   vanish), dilation with the 3 × 3 × 3 cube — the first version dilated with the cross too and counted the
   stair-step corners of smooth surfaces (0.6% of the teddy at N = 64, 0.14% at 128: the note fired on the preview
   and not on the final build; review). One `I_RECON_THIN` note per build when thin samples are ≥ 0.2% of the
   inside; per-part "crochet flat" flags belong to T3.3's parts and fit.
7. **Decimation target** = `min(cell.w, cell.h)/3` in inches (gauge cell), converted to world units with the MC
   mesh's height; `maxError` = half a voxel absolute. The result is rejected (input kept) unless watertight with no
   pinched vertex and no zero-area triangle; then manifold-3d validates it, and if that fails the undecimated mesh
   is validated instead. Many N = 128 meshes are already at the target density (the sphere test: 0.9-voxel edges =
   0.071 in vs 0.067 in target) and are left alone.
8. **Inches and frames.** The final mesh's bounds set the scale (`targetHeightIn / height`; single top photo:
   `/ max(width, depth)`, since `targetHeightIn` is then "the longest extent in the photo plane") and the shift
   (lowest point y = 0). x and z are not recentered: the multi-view origin is the object's box center from the
   alignment, and for side photos the photo plane stays the symmetry plane x = 0 (§2.9.3). The mesh part is
   part-local around its bbox center (`part.position` = that center, `dims.bboxIn` = its size), and the stored
   `sdf:<meshRef>` volume is in the same part-local inches (the frame `partSdf`/`inferAttach` take for mesh parts),
   cropped to the inside samples ± 2 (+∞ samples saturate).
9. **The model T3.2 returns**: one part `body` (`type: 'mesh'`, `stuffing: 'firm'`, `color: 'main'`), palette
   `[RECON_BASE_COLOR]`, `finishedSize` from the mesh, `source: { stage: 'recon', views: [labels] }`, name "Toy
   from photos" / "Toy from one photo", vertex labels 255, `labelImages` `{}`, `photoPalette` `[]`. `meshRef` =
   `recon_` + the first 12 hex digits of FNV-1a 64 over the part-local positions and indices (deterministic).
10. **`report.iouPerView`** is the IoU of the final volume (after rounding, before clean-up) projected onto each
    view's plane against that view's own table, on the build grid — how well the result matches each photo; below
    0.9 it raises `W_RECON_IOU` on that photo (`where.view`). The §2.9.2 badge (hull vs mask, `W_VIEW_IOU`) is
    computed at its own N = 128 (reusing the build's tables when N = 128, else `viewConsistency`), so the N = 64
    preview and the final build report the same badge; the one-plane path (front + back) checks it too.
11. **Failed builds reject** with `ReconError` (`E_VIEWS`; `E_MASK_EMPTY` for an empty single photo or when every
    photo is empty, with `where.view`; `E_RECON_EMPTY`; `E_RECON_INVALID`; `E_RECON_SIZE` when a part would exceed
    `MODEL_LIMITS.maxDimIn` = 48 in at the target size, e.g. a 7:1 rod from one side photo at 8 in high), because
    `ReconResult.model` must pass `validateModel`. A top photo whose companions are all empty is built alone as a
    single top photo (W_VIEW_EMPTY on the others). comlink
    passes only `name`, `message` and `stack` of an Error, so the message starts with the code and
    `reconErrorCode(e)` reads it back on the main thread (for F2 step 7's error panel). Malformed requests
    (N ∉ {64, 128, 192}, target ≤ 0 or > 60 in, κ ≤ 0, a mask of the wrong length, an invalid `align`, no views,
    a gauge cell ≤ 0) throw `RangeError`.
11a. **A single photo uses only `align.rot90` and `align.mirror`.** With one photo there is nothing to scale or
    shift it against, and an offset moved the photo plane off the symmetry plane x = 0 (review).
11b. **Cancellation granularity.** `gate.check` runs after the masks, the tables, the badge, the hull, each view's
    T, the rounding, the IoU, every phase of the clean-up (its flood fills yield every 2²⁰ visited samples), thin
    features, marching cubes, Taubin, decimation and validation. Measured with a timing gate on a loaded machine:
    ≤ 60 ms between checks at N = 128 and ≤ 80 ms at N = 192 (§5.8 asks for ≈ 50 ms; the first version had
    120–660 ms stretches; review). A perf test keeps the N = 128 worst stretch under 150 ms.
12. **Genus warning** only when `keepHoles` is off: with it on, a handle is the user's choice ("the user accepted a
    handle via keep holes", §2.9.5). With masks hole-filled, a hull handle rarely arises; the ring test checks the
    keepHoles path (genus 1, no warning).
13. **`useDepth` / `req.depth` and `req.views[].image`** are ignored in T3.2 (depth fusion is T3.4; photo colors
    T3.3).

## Review (T3.2)

adversarial inputs (full-frame, 1 px and line masks, checkerboards, rings, duplicate labels, front + back,

for every probe, Taubin +0.04…+0.34%, the stored SDF through `partSdf` (≤ 0.077 in at vertices, voxel 0.079),
memory (250 MB RSS at 128, 370 MB at 192), the worker transfer and the integration tasks. Fixed from its findings:

| Finding | Fix |
|---|---|
