goldens · T4.2 placement, BLO/jogless, text, validators · T4.3 frames, plan, trimming, ovals, cues, lean, assembly ·

## Deviations from the spec, with reasons

1. **Closed-end walk bounded by the ideals** (`closedEndRule`). §2.10.5 says "walking backwards from the last round
   down to the round with the largest count, `n_k = max(n_k, n_{k+1}, 5)`". Taken literally this fills every waist
   after the widest round: a snowman lathe (big ball, neck, small ball, closed top) gets its neck widened to the
   small ball's width (`6 12 18 24 18 12 18 12 6` → `… 24 18 18 18 12 6`). The walk now also stops, like the start
   rule, at the first round (from the end) whose ideal is ≥ the closing count. On every profile that only narrows
   after its peak — all DESIGN goldens, and 500 random single-bulge lathes in `rounds.test.ts` — both readings give
   identical counts. Request 1.
2. **Symmetric profiles keep their mirror through the pole rule.** §2.10.5 orders `mirrorHalf → clampPoles →
   clampFan` on the full list; a symmetric spindle (two cones base to base) then starts `5 5 6 …` but ends
   `… 6 5` (the trailing duplicate is dropped), and a classic profile whose first batched value is 12 starts at 6
   and ends at 12 — neither is symmetric, against the required property test. `pathACounts` runs round1 → start
   rule → fan on the first ceil(m/2) rounds and mirrors that; the far pole is the mirror of the start. Identical to
   the literal order on every golden (tested). Request 2.
3. **`closeTail` safety net** (not in the pseudocode). Batched classic counts lag a steep closing cap: with w/h 0.9
   (my random gauges; Table E gives 1.05 or 1.11) the ideal falls ~7 per round and `batchCounts` ends `… 24 18 12`,
   and the fan clamp can lift a last round above 8. The literal pseudocode then closes at 12 (`E_CLOSE`) on 337 of
   3000 random lathes (classic). `closeTail` appends halving rounds (12 → 6) until the circular end is ≤ 8, and sets an
   oval's last circular count to exactly 6 (§2.10.5 "ends with the circular part exactly 6"), lowering it when the
   fan allows, else appending. Counted in `PieceCounts.appended`; never fires for exact counts (0 / 3000). Request 3.
4. **Oval starts.** The widening clause "while `ideal_k < n₁`" is applied after a chain-oval start too (the spec
   names it for the magic ring only; E_START applies it generally). An oval piece whose first round has `S₁ = 0`
   starts on a magic ring with circular 6, not `clamp(round(ideal₁), 5, 8)`: its closed end must finish at
   circular 6, so any other start would break the symmetric-oval property (found by the property test:
   `rx 1.40, ry 0.25, rz 0.34` started at 5 and ended at 6). Request 4.
5. **At most one round dropped at a closed end.** §2.10.5's pseudocode says "drop trailing rounds equal to their
   predecessor"; its prose says "the piece closes one round early; R11 accepts the ≤ 1.5·hS loss at the tip". The
   literal loop deletes the whole straight tip of a long thin piece whose rounds were already equal (review: a
   lathe tail lost 20 of its rounds, ~4 in; a cone r 0.4, h 4 from its base lost 7 (exact) / 11 (classic)).
   `closedEndRule` drops one round, and only one the rule itself raised (the horn's `… 6 5 5` → `… 6 5`). Request 6.
6. **Classic batching after a chain-ring start starts from the chain count** (`batchCounts` p0 = n₀, not 6): from 6 a
   straight open tube of 31 chains batched `12 18 24 30 …`. Request 7.
7. **Round 1 of a magic ring / chain oval is never BLO/FLO**: a corner within half a round of the start (a tube
   ⌀0.2, a box 0.2 × 0.2) moves its BLO round to round 2. Request 8.
8. **Mirroring needs two closed poles.** Rounds k ↔ m + 1 − k sit at s and L − s only when m = N − 1; an open tube
   (m = N) is not mirrored (its counts come out symmetric from the profile anyway).
9. **Flattened pieces close at ≥ 4** (their ring may hold 4), so a symmetric flattened piece stays palindromic.
10. **Tie-safe rounding.** Every rounding uses `roundHalfUp` of `core/gauge` (s0b-gauge request 4): `N = round(L/hS)`
   for the cylinder is the tie 17.5 → 18.

### T4.2

11. **Rotation inside an arc, not a printed segment.** §2.10.8: "the rotations below then act inside one segment".
    The end that holds the round start prints as two segments (its first stitches open the round, the rest close
    it, G8 `inc | 7 sc | 3 inc | 7 sc | 2 inc`). It is one physical end, so it is placed as one arc (§2.10.8 base and
    rotation) and then cut at the round start; placing the two halves apart would bunch the specials at the seam.
    G8 Rnds 2–3 are identical either way. Request 12.
12. **End split, side transfers, round-start cut (not in the spec).** The circular change is split between the two
    end arcs: no inc3/dec3 when avoidable, then balanced ends, then the fewest changes (an end never hands a
    stitch to the other), then the odd stitch to end A on odd rounds, end B on even ones. A side growing from 0 sts
    gets its stitches from the end before it (that end makes them); a side shrinking to 0 is worked into that end.
    A decrease never straddles the round start: the arc is rotated until a cut exists, else its two parts are
    placed apart, else the next split / the other neighbor is tried, and as a last resort (2-st ends at a tiny
    open edge) the cut goes to the nearest op boundary. Request 12.
13. **Plain oval rounds print without segment hints** (`25 sc, sl st`, not `3 sc, 5 sc, 8 sc, 5 sc, 4 sc, sl st`);
    shaped oval rounds keep them, so sides stay readable as G8 asks.
14. **W_RUFFLE is literal** (`|n_k − n_{k−1}|` on the stated count): on an oval tip the side growth adds 2ΔS, so the
    teddy muzzle's Rnd 3 (14 → 22) warns. Request 10.
