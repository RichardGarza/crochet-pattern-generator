## Deviations from the spec, with reasons

1. **Closed-end walk bounded by the ideals** (`closedEndRule`). §2.10.5 says "walking backwards from the last round
   down to the round with the largest count, `n_k = max(n_k, n_{k+1}, 5)`". Taken literally this fills every waist
   after the widest round: a snowman lathe (big ball, neck, small ball, closed top) gets its neck widened to the
   small ball's width (`6 12 18 24 18 12 18 12 6` → `… 24 18 18 18 12 6`). The walk now also stops, like the start
   rule, at the first round (from the end) whose ideal is ≥ the closing count. On every profile that only narrows
   after its peak — all DESIGN goldens, and 500 random single-bulge lathes in `rounds.test.ts` — both readings give
   identical counts. Request 1.
2. **Symmetric profiles keep their mirror through the pole rule.** §2.10.5 orders `mirrorHalf → clampPoles →
   clampFan` on the full list; a symmetric spindle (two cones base to base) then starts `5 5 6 …` but ends
   `… 6 5` (the trailing duplicate is dropped), and a classic profile whose first batched value is 12 starts at 6
   and ends at 12 — neither is symmetric, against the required property test. `pathACounts` runs round1 → start
   rule → fan on the first ceil(m/2) rounds and mirrors that; the far pole is the mirror of the start. Identical to
   the literal order on every golden (tested). Request 2.
3. **`closeTail` safety net** (not in the pseudocode). Batched classic counts lag a steep closing cap: with w/h 0.9
   (my random gauges; Table E gives 1.05 or 1.11) the ideal falls ~7 per round and `batchCounts` ends `… 24 18 12`,
   and the fan clamp can lift a last round above 8. The literal pseudocode then closes at 12 (`E_CLOSE`) on 337 of
   3000 random lathes (classic). `closeTail` appends halving rounds (12 → 6) until the circular end is ≤ 8, and sets an
   oval's last circular count to exactly 6 (§2.10.5 "ends with the circular part exactly 6"), lowering it when the
   fan allows, else appending. Counted in `PieceCounts.appended`; never fires for exact counts (0 / 3000). Request 3.
4. **Oval starts.** The widening clause "while `ideal_k < n₁`" is applied after a chain-oval start too (the spec
   names it for the magic ring only; E_START applies it generally). An oval piece whose first round has `S₁ = 0`
   starts on a magic ring with circular 6, not `clamp(round(ideal₁), 5, 8)`: its closed end must finish at
   circular 6, so any other start would break the symmetric-oval property (found by the property test:
   `rx 1.40, ry 0.25, rz 0.34` started at 5 and ended at 6). Request 4.
5. **At most one round dropped at a closed end.** §2.10.5's pseudocode says "drop trailing rounds equal to their
   predecessor"; its prose says "the piece closes one round early; R11 accepts the ≤ 1.5·hS loss at the tip". The
   literal loop deletes the whole straight tip of a long thin piece whose rounds were already equal (review: a
   lathe tail lost 20 of its rounds, ~4 in; a cone r 0.4, h 4 from its base lost 7 (exact) / 11 (classic)).
   `closedEndRule` drops one round, and only one the rule itself raised (the horn's `… 6 5 5` → `… 6 5`). Request 6.
6. **Classic batching after a chain-ring start starts from the chain count** (`batchCounts` p0 = n₀, not 6): from 6 a
   straight open tube of 31 chains batched `12 18 24 30 …`. Request 7.
7. **Round 1 of a magic ring / chain oval is never BLO/FLO**: a corner within half a round of the start (a tube
   ⌀0.2, a box 0.2 × 0.2) moves its BLO round to round 2. Request 8.
8. **Mirroring needs two closed poles.** Rounds k ↔ m + 1 − k sit at s and L − s only when m = N − 1; an open tube
   (m = N) is not mirrored (its counts come out symmetric from the profile anyway).
9. **Flattened pieces close at ≥ 4** (their ring may hold 4), so a symmetric flattened piece stays palindromic.
10. **Tie-safe rounding.** Every rounding uses `roundHalfUp` of `core/gauge` (s0b-gauge request 4): `N = round(L/hS)`
   for the cylinder is the tie 17.5 → 18.

## Ambiguities resolved

| Where | Reading |
|---|---|
| §2.10.5 "symmetric profile (r(s) ≈ r(L − s) within 1%)" | Within 1% of the largest radius, at 257 evenly spaced positions; oval side widths too |
| §2.10.5 G6 "Lathe semicircle r = 1.5" | A `lathe` part with a 64-point semicircle (the schema maximum); the analytic sphere (exact) gives the same list. The classic list needs a lathe: a classic `sphere` uses the textbook path |
| §2.10.4 lathe ends off the axis | Closed by a flat disc, as the SDF closes it (s0b-model deviation 3); the junction is a corner when the turn ≥ 45° |
| §2.10.4 lathe `sharp` | Indices of profile points; a marked vertex is a corner whatever its turn |
| §2.10.5 "untrimmed open end … where the far cap begins" for the generic path | `openFar` drops the far cap: sphere/ellipsoid → the half towards the start, capsule → its far quarter circle, cylinder/cone(from the apex)/box/lathe → the far disc (only a disc the profile closes with, i.e. an off-axis end). A cone started from its base keeps its apex closed |
| §2.10.2 rule 1 `crochet.axis` | Honored for sphere, ellipsoid and box; capsule, cylinder, cone, lathe and torus are revolved about their local Y and keep it |
| §2.10.5 BLO "first round with s_k ≥ s_c + 0.5·hEff" | `ceil((s_c + 0.5·hEff)/hEff − 1e-9)`; a corner whose round lies past the last round is ignored; corners never sit at s = 0 or L |
| Corner kind | Convex when the tangent turns toward the axis side (`dz₁·dr₂ − dr₁·dz₂ < 0`): a cylinder rim; concave (FLO) for a step outwards |
| §2.10.5 "trailing rounds equal to their predecessor" | At most one, raised by the rule (deviation 5), on the circular part; an oval's `S` list is cut to match |
| `openFar` on a lathe that already returns to the axis, or a cone from its base | No cap to leave off: stays closed and gathered |
| `trimAt` ≥ the profile length | No cut (both paths); `trimAt` ≤ 1e-9, NaN or ∞ throws `RangeError` (both paths) |
| Degenerate profiles | Every point on the axis, an empty lathe, non-finite dims: `RangeError`; a zero-height fold back (180°) is a convex rim |
| Open start (cylinder open at its start) | Chain ring of `round(2π·r(0)/wS)` chains, no pole clamp (§2.10.5 "chain-ring start: no clamp") |
| §2.10.4 torus `n_k` | Rounds k = 0 … K − 1 (K rounds; round 1 = n₀ worked into the chain ring of n₀), then seamed; reproduces research 03 §5's `31 33 36 … 33`; the fan clamp applies; K ≥ 3 |
| §2.10.6 "36-st opening → 30" (75 cm)" | Holds at the firm gauge (wS 0.20475): 3 × 7.371 + 6 = 28.1 → 30. At the light gauge it is 28" (70 cm) |
| §2.10.6 closed sewn piece tail | `6 + T` printed without cm, exactly as the spec's sentence; `sewTailIn = T` |
| §2.10.6 flatten finish tail | `tailIn` 6 (no sewing tail unless T4.3 decides the piece is sewn) |
| Textbook `L` / `hEff` | `hEff = hS`; `L` = A (closed) or s_end (open), for the W_SIZE check of T4.2 |

## Requests for integration

1. **§2.10.5 pole rule, closed far end:** add the bound "and while `ideal_k < n_last`" to the backward walk (or
   otherwise restrict it to the closing cap) so a waist after the widest round is not filled (deviation 1). All
   goldens are unchanged.
2. **§2.10.5 symmetric profiles:** state that the pole rule and the fan clamp run on the first half before mirroring
   (or that the far pole mirrors the start), so "a symmetric profile gives symmetric counts" holds for spindles and
   for classic profiles whose first batched value exceeds 6 (deviation 2).
3. **§2.10.5 / §2.13 E_CLOSE:** the batched classic path can end above 8 at a closed end (`… 18 12`); say what the
   generator does — `closeTail` appends halving rounds — or bound the batched step at closing caps (deviation 3).
   The same paragraph says an oval's circular part ends "exactly 6"; nothing in the pseudocode enforces it.
4. **§2.10.5 pole rule for ovals:** the magic-ring start of an oval piece (`S₁ = 0`) — 6, as implemented, so that
   start and closed end agree; and whether the widening clause applies after a chain-oval start (deviation 4).
5. **§2.10.6 tail examples:** name the gauge of the 36-st example (firm: 30"; light: 28").
6. **§2.10.5 closed far end:** make the pseudocode match the prose — drop at most one trailing round, one the rule
   raised (deviation 5).
7. **§2.10.5 `batchCounts` p0:** p0 = the chain count after a chain-ring start (deviation 6).
8. **§2.10.5 BLO/FLO:** say that round 1 of a magic ring / chain oval is never a BLO/FLO round (deviation 7).
9. **§2.10.2 `crochet.axis`** on capsule, cylinder, cone, lathe and torus is ignored (they are revolved about local
   Y); consider a warning when it is set to something else, or say so in §3.5.1.
