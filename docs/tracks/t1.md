Tests (`src/core/image2d/__tests__/`, node environment, synthetic images from `src/test/rgba.ts` plus
`__tests__/images.ts`): 107 tests in 6 files — `linear`, `crop`, `kind`, `background`, `labels`, `sample`.

An independent review (adversarial subagent) found 16 issues; fixed in this sprint: thin outlines breaking
apart at cell sizes above ~12 px (relative claim threshold + gap joining), graph paper / 1-px lines on a lattice
read as pixel art and erased (margin-blend check), noisy pixel art with pure black rejected (dark-chroma
damping), one stray transparent pixel switching to alpha mode (1% rule), missing grid warnings on the pixel
path, stale cached stats accepted for a same-size rotation (fingerprint), O(n²) period search on a long axis
(coarse-to-fine), full-size float copy of large photos (row-streamed downscale), no seam for caching the
working image (`prepareWork` / `sampleCells` / `prepareKey`), the thin mask covering dithers and noise
(texture and speck exclusion), bridging stealing another feature's cell, negative/sliver crops, unchecked spans
in `poolLabels`, a background color reported when none was found. Not changed: the review's notes 12 and 16
(see "Known limits").
No PNG fixtures were committed: every picture is generated in code (a PNG round trip through
`core/kernel/png.ts` is tested in `sample.test.ts`).

## Decisions and resolved ambiguities

1. **Crop convention** (the frozen `CropRect` does not say): `x, y, w, h` in decoded-image pixels before the
   rotation, rounded to whole pixels and clipped; `rotate` is clockwise (CSS); `flipX` mirrors after rotating.
2. **Pixel-art period search.** §2.3.4 says "autocorrelation period ≥ 3 px gives block size s and phase". The
   detector uses the comb (lattice) form of the autocorrelation — for each period, the share of the edge evidence
   that one lattice `phase + k·s` explains within 1.25 px — because it returns the phase directly and handles
   fractional periods (art scaled by a non-integer factor, e.g. 32 → 250 px). Details: noise floor from the
   profile's median + 3·MAD; a blurred boundary (a run of positions, split at valleys) counts as one edge at its
   centroid; the coverage is chance-corrected (a finer lattice catches more evidence by accident); the coarsest
   period within 0.03 of the best wins (divisors tie with the true period, multiples miss lines), refined to the
   middle of its plateau or a whole number on it.
3. **Pixel-art acceptance** adds four guards to the spec's "≥ 90% of blocks with ΔEOKr2 std < 0.03", because
   that test alone accepts any lattice on a picture with large flat areas (a flag, a logo): per axis, ≥ 75% of the
   cleaned edge evidence on the lattice, ≥ 60% of the raw edge counts (above the median) within a pixel of it,
   ≥ 3 lines with an edge, and ≥ 50% of the lattice lines between the first and last edge carrying one; plus
   8–1000 blocks per side. The block std is measured on the block interior (a margin of ⌊s/4⌋ px, which
   resampling blur and JPEG ringing smear across boundaries). Uniform noise of ±8 code values per channel is
   about the limit the 0.03 std threshold tolerates on 8 px blocks.
3a. **Dark chroma.** OKLab's a, b come out of cube roots and are unstable near black: ±4 code values of noise
   on #000000 move them by ΔEOKr2 ≈ 0.05 (≈ ΔE00 1.5), and every black block of noisy pixel art failed the 0.03
   std test. The detector's features (edges, flatness, block test — nothing else) scale the chroma down linearly
   below toe lightness 0.2 (`DARK_CHROMA_L`). Above it the spec's distances are unchanged.
3b. **Margins must be blends.** The block test also requires the margin pixels (skipped by the std test) to be
   blends of the block and a neighbor: fewer than max(2, s/2) margin pixels may lie farther than ΔEOKr2 0.1
   from every segment between the block's mean and a neighbor's. Otherwise 1-px lines on every lattice line
   (graph paper, gingham, tables) hid in the margins and were erased. Occupancy threshold raised to 0.6 after
   review (a 30-px-squares / 10-px-gaps logo passed at 0.51).
3c. **Period search cost.** Coarse pass with steps that drift ≤ 1/8 px over min(n, 2048) px, then a refine on
   the full-resolution grid within ±5% of the winner (a 12000 × 40 strip: 1.8 s → < 0.3 s).
4. **Native-resolution art** (period 1–2) is not detected (the spec's period ≥ 3); it comes out as flat art. When
   the user picks "pixel art" for a picture without a lattice, every pixel is a cell (if ≤ 1000 per side; else
   flat art with `W_PIXEL_UNAVAILABLE`). A detected lattice that failed only the block test is used for the
   override.
5. **Pixel-art size.** One cell per native pixel; `W_GRID_LARGE` above 300 cells a side and `W_GRID_NO_ROOM` /
   `W_PIXEL_SIZE` for a size ≤ 0, as on the other paths; with a requested size, each native pixel becomes m × m cells,
   m = the whole multiple nearest to the requested width (or height when only a height is given), capped at
   1000 cells. `W_PIXEL_SIZE` when m > 1 or the result is off the request by > 2.5%; `W_PIXEL_MULTIPLE` when a
   technique count rule (mosaic 12n + 3) is broken; `I_PIXEL_ASPECT` when non-square stitches change the
   proportions by > 2.5%. Cell colors are the block-interior averages.
6. **Default size.** With neither width nor height, the chart is `DEFAULT_WIDTH_STITCHES` = 60 stitches wide
   (+ border) and an `I_SIZE_DEFAULT` note says so (`grid` throws without a size). T2's settings presumably
   always pass one.
7. **Flat-art "3×3 median on labels"** is a conservative mode filter (a pixel changes only when its label fills
   ≤ 2 of its 9-pixel window): a majority filter erases 1-px lines before thin-feature protection can keep them.
   It runs after quantization (labels exist only then).
8. **Thin features.** 8-connected components; distance transform = distance to the nearest pixel with another
   label in its 4-neighborhood, + 1; "half a cell" = half the larger cell side in px; skeleton = the ridge of that
   distance; coverage = the component's share of the cell area; a cell two thin components claim goes to the
   larger coverage. Beyond the spec's diagonal bridge, a skeleton cell that joins two otherwise separate groups of
   the feature's cells is claimed too: where a 2-px line crosses a cell corner its coverage splits and neither
   cell reaches 15%. The 15% threshold itself is lowered, per component and cell, to the coverage of a crossing
   of half the cell's smaller side (width ≈ 2·area / exposed sides): a 2-px line crossing a 15-px cell straight
   covers 13%, and with a fixed 15% a 1-px line vanishes from every cell above 6.7 px — the spec's 15% alone
   broke 2-px circles into 6–8 pieces at 12–20 px cells (review). Textures are excluded (a component covering
   > 40% of the cells it touches: dithers, fine checkers, label noise) and so are specks (shorter than a cell).
   A bridge never takes another thin feature's cell.
9. **Background.** Flood tolerance is measured to the ring cluster's mean (pixel-to-pixel creeps along
   gradients); a barrier pixel within tolerance is filled but does not spread (the fill reaches the subject's
   edge without crossing it); barriers need Sobel ≥ max(P90, 0.2) — in a mostly flat picture P90 is 0. "Alpha
   present" means ≥ 1% of the pixels have α < 0.5; otherwise the picture has no background at all (no
   background cells; translucent or stray transparent pixels are composited over the background color), and
   "remove" still runs the plain-background fill. When no plain background is found, the reported color is
   white (`hexFrom: 'white'`), not the ring color. "Remove" on a transparent picture uses the transparency
   (`I_BG_TRANSPARENT`). The background color defaults to the ring color (plain) or white (transparent) until
   T1.2 maps it to the nearest palette yarn / the reference line's white.
10. **Issue codes** added (not in the §2.13 table): `W_BG_NOT_FOUND`, `W_BG_SUBJECT_SMALL`,
    `W_BG_SUBJECT_LARGE`, `I_BG_TRANSPARENT`, `W_PIXEL_SIZE`, `W_PIXEL_MULTIPLE`, `W_PIXEL_UNAVAILABLE`,
    `I_PIXEL_ASPECT`, `I_SIZE_DEFAULT`; plus the S0 `W_GRID_*` codes passed through from `gridIssues`.
11. **Crops.** A negative `w`/`h` extends left/up; a crop that overlaps the image keeps at least one pixel per
    axis; a crop that misses it throws.
12. **Cached stats** carry `imageFingerprint` (size + 4096 × 64 sampled pixels); `prepareImage` refuses stats
    whose fingerprint differs from the crop's (a same-size rotation, flip or moved crop).

### T1.2 decisions

13. **Brush semantics** (§2.3.2 leaves the fill interaction open). Brushed subject pixels are never filled and stop
    the flood fill like a wall; brushed background pixels are background and let a fill that reaches them pass,
    but do not start one (an enclosed hole is background exactly where it was brushed; a brushed channel from the
    border opens it). Without a plain background (ring test failed) the brushed pixels alone are the background,
    worked in their mean color (`hexFrom: 'brush'`), and `W_BG_NOT_FOUND` is not raised. On a transparent picture
    brushed background pixels join the transparency; a brushed-subject transparent pixel has no color and stays
    background. A malformed brush (data length ≠ w·h) is treated as stale. The brush is ignored with "keep" (no
    stale warning either).
14. **Content id.** The fallback cache id hashes every pixel with two 32-bit murmur-style lanes (byte-wise FNV-1a
    took ≈ 1 s on 12 MP; this takes ≈ 30 ms). It is a cache key, not a content address.
15. **Anti-aliasing centers (flat art).** Kneedle's normalized curve always scores K = 2 at 0, so on an
    anti-aliased two-color logo the knee keeps 3–5 "colors" that are edge blends, and G14 cannot pass with §2.4.3
    alone. A center is removed as a blend when its color is within ΔEOKr2 0.05 of a 5–95% mix of two other
    centers — mixed in linear light or in sRGB (most renderers anti-alias in sRGB; the logo fixture does) — and at
    least half of its pixels have both of those centers within 2 px. Every qualifying pair is tried (an end may
    itself be a blend not yet removed); the most edge-bound center goes first, then all pixels are reassigned.
    Protected centers are never removed; photos are not touched (their mid-tones are real). A real third color
    is kept (test). A 1–3 px line in a color that happens to lie between its two neighbours' colors would be
    removed — accepted.
16. **Exact K.** Before the knee, auto-K takes the smallest K whose D(K) is 0 (at most Kmax distinct colors:
    pixel art, clean graphics); the knee would drop a real color there (3 exact colors give the curve [D, 0],
    whose knee is K = 2).
17. **Pooled points.** The D(K) curve, and p-median's bins (§2.4.4 "Σ_bins"), use the points pooled into ΔEOKr2
    0.02 cubes (1 JND) when there are more than 4096 of them; the chosen K is then quantized on the real points
    (photo: one per distinct cell, as §2.4.2 says). The variance-split sequence is computed once for every K
    (the splits are nested). Photo 200 × 200: 370 → 77 ms.
18. **p-median starts.** SWAP runs from BUILD and from both snap-after-quantize sets (snapping by ΔEOKr2 and by
    ΔE00); the cheapest wins, so "costs ≤ snap-after-quantize" holds by construction. Distance = ΔEOKr2 (§2.4.4).
19. **Color budget.** "Kmax" of the salience/override rules is the budget in force: `maxColors` when it is a
    number (clamped to 1…technique max), else the technique's max K. The background is outside it. Hand-edit
    colors are kept even beyond it (`W_OVERRIDE_COLORS`); salient groups get at most `cap − 1 − protected`
    centers (largest groups first).
20. **Salience details.** Neighboring salient cells join a group only when alike (ΔE00 < 10), so a red eye next
    to a blue one is two groups; groups within ΔE00 5 share one center (two eyes, one red). Cells that are a mix
    of two current centers (`isMixOf`: a box average straddling an edge) are not salient — otherwise every
    anti-aliased edge of a logo or a photo became a "detail". A cell given a textured yarn is measured against the
    nearest solid chosen yarn. Salient cells are then forced to their protected center (as hand edits are), and
    their pixels survive the flat-art despeckle (`despeckleLabels(…, keep)`). In yarn modes a salient group adds
    its nearest SOLID candidate (ΔE00) as a fixed member and p-median is rerun; in auto mode the group mean is
    added and the least populous unprotected center dropped (§2.4.3).
21. **Merge rule** applies to free centers (auto mode) right after quantizing; yarn modes rely on p-median (two
    chosen yarns closer than ΔE00 5 lower its cost, so they are kept).
22. **Hand edits (§5.5.5).** Identity = lowercase hex + yarn id. Auto mode: an override within ΔE00 < 2 of a free
    center (or one with the same yarn id as an inserted override) maps to it and makes it protected; otherwise its
    exact hex is inserted (`role: 'override'`, named by its own yarn when the id resolves, else the reference
    line). Yarn modes: its own yarn when the candidates have it, else the nearest shade (ΔE00), fixed in
    p-median. Overridden cells take the override's entry whatever the picture says. Edits made at another size
    are remapped by relative cell position (`remapEdits`, `I_EDITS_REMAPPED`) — the confirm dialog is T2's;
    edits outside the chart or without a valid hex are dropped (`W_EDITS_INVALID`). Locked cells join `protect`.
23. **Background yarn (§2.3.2).** Auto mode: the chosen `backgroundColor`, else the border color (plain), else the
    reference line's white (transparent: nearest shade to #ffffff, RHSS White #ffffff). Yarn modes: the chosen
    yarn when the candidates have its id, else the nearest candidate to the chosen/border/white color. When the
    hex changes, the cells are re-composited over it. A color entry of the same yarn (yarn modes) or an
    unprotected center within ΔE00 5 (auto) shares the background's entry (`role: 'background'`, the background
    hex wins) — one white, not two.
24. **Naming.** Auto entries carry `yarn` = the nearest reference shade (ΔE00; textured shades skipped for
    protected entries) and `name` = its name, + " (approximate)" when ΔE00 > 10. Yarn-mode entries are the
    yarn (hex and name); `deltaE00` = ΔE00 between the mean color of the entry's cells and the yarn.
25. **Codes** are `A…Z, AA, AB…` in descending population (ties: first-found order), background included.
26. **Line and yarn ids.** RHSS is `red-heart-super-saver` (makebead's id); yarn ids `<lineId>:<number>`; CSV ids
    `custom:<rrggbb>:<name-slug>` (stable when rows move). `nearestYarn` searches only shipped lines (its
    signature has no line data); `colorize` searches the request's lines first.
27. **Textured shades** are recognised by name (heather, marl, fleck, tweed, …) at import and in CSVs.
28. **Licence gate in code.** `import-yarns.mjs` accepts only CC-BY-4.0 and CC0-1.0 and needs source, licence,
    licence evidence, retrieval date, attribution and the changes made (CC BY's "indicate if changes were made");
    inputs are pinned by sha256; `--check` proves the shipped JSON is reproducible.

### Known limits

- Grid-aligned flat art with enough edges on a fine lattice can still pass as pixel art; its chart is then a
  faithful staircase at that lattice, but the requested size is ignored (with `W_PIXEL_SIZE`). The user can
  override the kind.
- Pixel art with fewer than 8 native pixels per side (or blocks above 256 px) locks onto a divisor of the block
  size: a 7 × 7 sprite ×8 becomes 14 × 14 cells (each native pixel 2 × 2). Faithful, but at 2×.
- Smooth gradients (synthetic, noise-free) score flatness ≈ 0.99 and are "flat art" — the spec's rule 2 as
  written; real photos have noise and come out as photos.
- `analyzeImage` and `prepareWork` are synchronous: on a 12 MP photo they take ≈ 0.7 s and ≈ 0.4 s, longer
  than the ≈ 50 ms between `gate.check` calls of §5.8. T1.4 caches both (they do not depend on the size or
  colors) and, if needed, chunks the row loops behind an async check.
- (T1.2) A logo with sensor/JPEG noise strong enough to be classified as a photo keeps its anti-aliasing shades
  as colors (blend removal runs on flat art only; a photo's mid-tones are real). Choosing "flat art" fixes it;
  T1.3's cleanup may absorb them too.
- (T1.2) Flat-art pooling at the 2048 px analysis size costs ≈ 0.3–0.4 s per assignment (most of it the
  thin-feature protection of `poolLabels`: components and a distance transform over 3 MP). `colorize` assigns
  twice at most when blends are removed; T1.4 should profile `protectThin` against the 300 ms budget.
- (T1.2) Yarn hex values are the source's uncalibrated approximations ("approximate" upstream); the materials page
  must say so (§2.4.4).
